How to use Count-In

Open a song, check the orange “1”, pick a count-in and save. Everything else is optional; this page walks through it.

First downbeat and tempo

  • The orange “1” marker sits on the first downbeat. The count-in ends right before it.
  • If the detector got it wrong, drag the marker. It snaps to the detected beats; ⌥ turns snapping off.
  • The arrows next to the time move the “1” one beat earlier or later.
  • Set the tempo with − and +, by typing a number, or with Tap: the song plays and you tap Space.
  • ×2 and ÷2 help when the detector counted twice as fast or slow.
  • The orange “The detector found another “1”” banner shows up when it is unsure. Click it to take the other candidate.

Tempo by ear (Tap)

  • The Tap button in the Tempo card opens the “Tempo by ear” window, and the song starts playing just before the “1”.
  • Tap Space along with the beat, at least three times. The taps are timed by the computer clock, not the audio buffer, so the tempo is right to a tenth of a BPM.
  • Stop: two beats after your last tap the song goes on with a click at the tapped tempo and phase, so you hear at once whether it matches the song.
  • If it drifts, just tap again: a gap of more than two seconds starts over.
  • Apply puts the tapped tempo into the Tempo card; Cancel or closing the window keeps the old one. Either way the music stops.

Count-in

  • “Even” clicks on every beat, “Build-up” starts sparse and gets denser, MIDI takes the pattern from your file.
  • The count-in lasts one or two bars; the meter is 4/4, 3/4 or 6/8.
  • The count-in volume does not depend on the song's level.

Playback and navigation

  • Space starts and stops playback. Without a playhead it starts from the count-in.
  • A click on the waveform sets the playhead, and playback starts from it. A double-click plays at once from the bar before that point.
  • The ◀◀ and ▶▶ buttons and the ← and → keys jump to the previous and next pause and play from the bar before it. Home goes back to the count-in.
  • The overview under the waveform shows the whole song, the pauses and the visible part. Drag the frame to scroll; a click outside it moves the view there.
  • The mouse wheel zooms; dragging and sideways trackpad scrolling pan the waveform.
  • The playback volume in the window header never reaches the saved file.

Pauses

  • A pause is a run of bars that are quiet once the removed instruments are gone. A hi-hat plays on every beat in it. The stick count-in plays only at the start of the song.
  • With “Fill pauses with hi-hat” on, the app finds pauses by itself in the track without the parts you turned down. “Silence threshold” gets fitted to it automatically; move it and the pauses rebuild. Turning a part back on keeps the pauses.
  • Pause edges drag along the bars. The current pause is brighter.
  • A new pause comes from the + button in “Pause fill” on the bar under the playhead, from selecting bars with ⇧ or from dragging along the time ruler.
  • The cross in its header, the − button in “Pause fill” or a right-click removes a pause.
  • A click on a pause header opens its own tempo and meter.

Backing track

  • The guitar, vocals, bass, drums and piano sliders turn a part down or remove it. The speaker button mutes a part in one click.
  • The song separates into parts in the background after it opens. Anything that needs the parts starts by itself once they are ready.
  • Levels can change while the song plays.
  • The floppy disk in the card header saves the current settings as defaults, the arrow next to it restores them.

Saving

  • “Save” writes the track with the count-in, the hi-hat in pauses and the chosen part levels.
  • Multitrack for a DAW writes a folder with the parts as WAV and the click grid and tempo map as MIDI. Every file starts on bar 1: put them at the project start and the DAW bars match the song.
  • By default the format matches the source. MP3 320 kbps, FLAC, ALAC and WAV are available; tags and cover art carry over.
  • “Trim leading silence” removes the empty space before the count-in.
  • The output folder and grouping by artist, year and album are set in Settings.

Models

  • Analysis and separation need models, about 780 MB. The app downloads them once on first launch.
  • An assets folder next to the app works too, so the models can travel with it on a flash drive.

If something goes wrong

  • Help → Report a Problem… sends your description to the author. If you agree, details about the computer and the app log go with it; no audio is sent, though the log may name the songs you opened.
