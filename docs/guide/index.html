How to use Count-In

Open a song, check the orange “1”, pick a count-in and save. Everything else is optional; this page walks through it.

First downbeat and tempo

  • The orange “1” marker sits on the first downbeat. The count-in ends right before it.
  • If the detector got it wrong, drag the marker. It snaps to the detected beats; ⌥ turns snapping off.
  • The arrows next to the time move the “1” one beat earlier or later.
  • Set the tempo with − and +, by typing a number, or with Tap. With Tap the song plays and you tap Space.
  • ×2 and ÷2 help when the detector counted twice as fast or slow.
  • The orange “The detector found another “1”” banner shows up when it is unsure. Click it to take the other candidate.
  • The line above the waveform shows the detected tempo, the place of the “1” and the song's key. The key is estimated from the spectrum, so check it by ear.
  • The round arrows in the card bring back the tempo and the “1” the detector found.

Tap tempo

  • The Tap button in the Tempo card opens the “Tap Tempo” window, and the song starts playing just before the “1”.
  • Tap Space along with the beat, at least three times. The taps are timed by the computer clock, so the tempo is right to a tenth of a BPM.
  • Stop, and two beats after your last tap the song carries on with a click at the tapped tempo and phase. You hear at once whether it matches the song.
  • If the click drifts from the song, tap again. A gap of more than two seconds starts over.
  • Apply puts the tapped tempo into the Tempo card; Cancel or closing the window keeps the old one. Either way the music stops.

Count-in

  • “Even” clicks on every beat. “Build-up” switches to eighths over the second half of the last bar and gets louder with every hit. MIDI takes the pattern from your file.
  • “Click through the whole song” carries the click on after the count-in, on every beat to the end of the song. The click sound and volume are set under the checkbox, and pause fill is not needed then.
  • The count-in lasts one or two bars; the meter is 4/4, 3/4 or 6/8.
  • The count-in volume does not depend on the song's level.
  • “Count-in bar when starting mid-song” adds a bar of clicks before the point where playback starts and before each new loop pass. It never reaches the saved file.

Playback and navigation

  • Space starts and stops playback. Without a playhead it starts from the count-in.
  • A click on the waveform sets the playhead, and playback starts from it. A double-click plays at once from the bar before that point.
  • While the song plays, a click on the waveform or the overview moves the sound there at once.
  • The ◀◀ and ▶▶ buttons and the ← and → keys jump to the previous and next pause and play from the bar before it. Home goes back to the count-in.
  • The overview under the waveform shows the whole song, the pauses and the visible part. Drag the frame to scroll; a click outside it moves the view there.
  • The mouse wheel zooms; dragging and sideways trackpad scrolling pan the waveform.
  • The ruler under the waveform counts bars. Go to Bar… in the Playback menu (⌘G, Ctrl+G on Windows) puts the playhead on the bar with that number.
  • Play Again (PageUp) goes back to the loop start, the playhead or the count-in.
  • The playback volume in the window header never reaches the saved file.

Loop and speed trainer

  • Drag across bars with ⇧ or along the ruler under the waveform and choose Play in a Loop. The selected bars repeat while the loop is on.
  • The L key and the loop button under the waveform turn it on and off.
  • Speed Trainer in the Playback menu adds 5% of speed every two laps until it reaches 100%. Slow the song down, turn the loop on and play along.

Speed and key

  • Speed under the waveform slows the song down to 50% in 5% steps with the pitch kept. The count-in and hi-hat follow the slower grid.
  • Below 80% the sound smears, which is fine for working out notes.
  • Key shifts the song by semitones, up to six up or down, with the tempo kept. −1 suits Eb tuning, −2 suits D.
  • Speed and key apply to playback and to the saved file.

Section markers

  • The M key adds a section marker at the playhead and asks for its name, such as Verse or Solo.
  • Keys 1–9 jump to the first nine markers.
  • A right-click on a marker's name on the ruler renames or removes it.
  • In a multitrack the markers go into the tempo map tempo.mid, and the DAW shows them on its ruler.

Pauses

  • A pause is a run of bars that are quiet once the removed instruments are gone. A hi-hat plays on every beat in it. The stick count-in plays only at the start of the song.
  • With “Fill pauses with hi-hat” on, the app finds pauses by itself in the track without the parts you turned down. “Silence threshold” gets fitted to it automatically; move it and the pauses rebuild. Turning a part back on keeps the pauses.
  • Pause edges drag along the bars. The current pause is brighter.
  • The + button in “Pause fill” makes a pause of the bar under the playhead. For several bars, drag across them with ⇧ or along the ruler and choose Make a Hi-Hat Pause.
  • The cross in its header, the − button in “Pause fill” or a right-click removes a pause.
  • A click on a pause header opens its own tempo and meter.
  • “Build-up at the end of a pause” turns the last bar of a pause into a lead-in to the entry, like the build-up count-in.

Backing track

  • The guitar, vocals, bass, drums and piano sliders turn a part down, remove it or boost it by up to 6 dB. The speaker button mutes a part in one click.
  • The S button plays only its own part. Several parts can be soloed at once. Solo does not change the saved file.
  • The song separates into parts in the background after it opens. Anything that needs the parts starts by itself once they are ready.
  • Levels can change while the song plays.
  • The floppy disk in the card header saves the current settings as defaults, the arrow next to it restores them.

Song memory and undo

  • The app remembers what you set for each song (tempo, the “1”, count-in, part levels, speed, key, click, pauses, loop and markers) and brings it back the next time the song opens.
  • ⌘Z undoes a change of the tempo, the “1”, pauses and markers, and ⇧⌘Z redoes it. On Windows these are Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y.
  • Recent songs are on the start screen and under File → Open Recent.

Set list and pedal

  • Open or drop several songs at once and they become a set list. The N and P keys open the next and the previous song.
  • A page-turner pedal sends PageDown and PageUp. PageDown starts and stops playback, PageUp plays again.

Saving

  • “Save” writes the track with the count-in, the hi-hat in pauses and the chosen part levels.
  • Multitrack for a DAW writes a folder with the parts as WAV, the click grid and tempo map as MIDI and, if you like, the click as a WAV of its own. Every file starts on bar 1. Put them at the project start and the DAW bars match the song.
  • By default the format matches the source. MP3 320 kbps, FLAC, ALAC and WAV are available; tags and cover art carry over.
  • “Even loudness” brings the file to −14 LUFS, as on streaming services, so backing tracks from different albums play equally loud. Peaks stay at −1 dB at most.
  • “Stage mix” puts the click in the left channel and the music, in mono, in the right. The click goes to the in-ears, the music to the front of house.
  • “Trim leading silence” removes the empty space before the count-in.
  • The output folder and grouping by artist, year and album are set in Settings.

Keys

  • Space and PageDown start and stop playback.
  • Home goes back to the count-in, PageUp plays again.
  • ← and → jump to the previous and next pause.
  • L toggles the loop, M adds a section marker, 1–9 jump to the markers.
  • N and P open the next and the previous song of the set list.
  • ⌘G goes to a bar, ⌘[ and ⌘] make it slower and faster.
  • ⌘Z undoes a change, ⇧⌘Z redoes it.
  • ⌘+ and ⌘− zoom the waveform, ⌘0 shows the whole track.
  • On Windows, Ctrl works in place of ⌘.

Models

  • Analysis and separation need models, about 780 MB. The app downloads them once on first launch.
  • An assets folder next to the app works too, so the models can travel with it on a flash drive.

If something goes wrong

  • Help → Report a Problem… sends your description to the author. If you agree, details about the computer and the app log go with it; no audio is sent, though the log may name the songs you opened.
